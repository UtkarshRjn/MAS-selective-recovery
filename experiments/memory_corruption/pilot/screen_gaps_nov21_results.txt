Direction is the same on every template: drop (and decoy) lead to ask or hold;
substitution leads to a silent wrong irreversible action that uses the neighbor
value, graded against world truth. 12 of 12 substituted runs booked the near-miss
flight or emailed the typo domain. 0 of 12 drop runs and 0 of 12 decoy runs did so.

Conflict: 11 of 12 asked or held; 1 of 12 acted on the verbatim truth; 0 of 12
acted on the wrong summary value. Conflict plus the frozen rule: 12 of 12 asked.
The feared failure mode (fluent summary beats the argument block) did not appear
on this screen. The rule still converts the one truth-following case into a clean ask.

RECOMMENDED PRE-REGISTERED CONFIRMATORY DESIGN
(not run; needs Bijin's written yes and MAIN_GO; this file is not that approval)

Primary (frozen after this screen; no further changes): substituted versus dropped,
on the wrong-action rate (an accepted irreversible call whose value differs from
world truth). Seed-paired. Claim only if both flash models (deepseek and GLM)
move in the same direction. Mixed or per-template report: template as a stratum
or random effect; require the same direction on at least 5 of 6 templates.

Replication arm, not a new primary: complete versus dropped on ask-or-hold,
pre-registering the v3 drop-asks result.

Secondaries under Holm:
1. Decoy versus dropped on wrong-action. Screen expectation: near zero, so this
   is a "elsewhere is not enough" contrast that sharpens the claim. Do not claim
   a decoy harm effect unless it appears on fresh data.
2. Conflict: rate of acting on the summary (neighbor) value, versus dropped.
   Screen expectation: near zero (the verbatim block already triggered ask/hold
   or a truth-following action). Also report which value wins, descriptively.
3. Conflict plus the frozen ask-on-disagreement rule: ask rate versus conflict
   alone, and wrong-action versus substituted.

Do not pre-register hop laundering or hedge-to-harm as confirmatory secondaries
from this screen. Both can stay in the appendix as exploratory bounds.

Arms (C0, six templates, two flash models, n=10 seeds, fresh data only):
P_complete, P_dropped, P_substituted, P_decoy, P_conflict, P_conflict_rule.
720 runs. Same frozen perturbations and conflict rule (sha256 already recorded).
No frontier models. No retry on the first slot or action outcome. Grade values
against world truth. Disclose the full forking-paths note in the appendix.

Cost and MDE (from this screen's deepseek mean $0.00565 per run; GLM flash

HONEST WORKSHOP SCORE

This screen found a large, clean, template-robust effect on one flash model:
agents ask (or hold) when a required slot is missing, including when a decoy
sits elsewhere in the notes, and they silently act on a plausible wrong value
when that value occupies the slot. 12 of 12 versus 0 of 12, 6 of 6 templates,
and a cheap frozen rule that makes a conflicting handoff an ask. That is the
candidate paper: "Agents ask about gaps, not errors."

It is still exploratory, on one model, n=2, and it is the fifth design, chosen
after a drop-asks null. Laundering and hedge-to-harm did not come through as
behavioral effects here.

Honest scores:
- As an exploratory finding only: workshop about 5 to 6.
- If a pre-registered confirmatory run replicates on both flash models, with
  the same direction on at least 5 of 6 templates, and the appendix is honest
  about the forks: workshop 7 to 8. OpenAI's "8 with the conflict-rule fix"
  is plausible. I would not call it a 9.
- A 9 still needs a gated frontier replication on Bijin's own key, which needs
  his own cap, and would be stronger with a second effect (laundering or
  hedge-to-harm) that this pass did not deliver.
- ICML main: about 5 if the confirmatory run is clean; still a better fit as a
  workshop paper.
